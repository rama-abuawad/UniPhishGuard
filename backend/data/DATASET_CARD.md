# UniPhishGuard Training Dataset Card

## Summary

- **Name:** UniPhishGuard consolidated training dataset
- **File:** `training_dataset.csv`
- **Purpose:** Train and internally evaluate the English-language phishing-risk text classifier used by UniPhishGuard.
- **Records:** 11,000 total: 6,500 labelled `phishing` and 4,500 labelled `legitimate`.
- **Current columns:** `label`, `text`.

## Provenance and licences

Repository history shows that the first 1,000 records were generated by the former `backend/generate_training_data.py` script: 500 legitimate and 500 phishing university-style messages produced from 40 templates per class with small substitutions. These records are synthetic. The repository does not contain a licence statement for these generated examples.

The remaining 10,000 records were previously stored as `phishing_legit_dataset_KD_10000.csv` and were described only as an additional “real-style” labelled dataset. No original publisher, download URL, collection method, licence, or redistribution permission is recorded in the repository history. It is therefore not possible to verify whether individual records are collected, transformed, or synthetic.

**Source or licence requires verification before public redistribution.**

The dataset must not be presented as wholly real-world data. Public distribution should be paused until the external portion's provenance, licence, and privacy status are documented.

## Labelling and processing

The generated portion received labels from the template category. The labelling process for the other 10,000 records is not documented and requires verification. The consolidation step converted labels to `legitimate` and `phishing` and combined subject/body fields for the generated portion.

The current training script normalizes whitespace and case for exact-duplicate removal. The baseline audit found 964 duplicate rows across 124 duplicated normalized-text groups, leaving 10,036 unique records. Earlier evaluation used a random row split after exact deduplication; template-like near duplicates could therefore cross partitions. The improved training workflow must use group-aware splitting and report leakage checks.

## Coverage, privacy, ethics, and bias

Current language coverage is primarily English. Arabic and mixed Arabic-English phishing detection have not been evaluated. The generated portion emphasizes university account, tuition, scholarship, helpdesk, and Microsoft 365 themes. This can bias the classifier toward those phrases and may increase false positives for legitimate university notices.

The dataset should not contain credentials, access tokens, or private mailbox content. Because the external portion's collection process is unknown, its privacy status requires review before redistribution or production training. Labels can be wrong, dated, or context-dependent. A model prediction is risk evidence, not proof of malicious intent.

## Intended uses

- Undergraduate research and controlled demonstrations.
- Training an explainable TF-IDF and Logistic Regression baseline.
- Internal comparison of model versions using leakage-aware splits.
- Supporting, not replacing, user and IT review.

## Uses not recommended

- Autonomous blocking, deletion, or quarantine of email.
- Claims that a low-risk result guarantees safety.
- Production deployment without representative external university-email validation.
- Arabic or multilingual performance claims.
- Automatic retraining from unreviewed user feedback.

## Known limitations

- Unverified source and licence for 10,000 records.
- Strong template repetition and near-duplicate risk.
- Primarily English coverage.
- Limited sender, header, attachment, and organizational context in text-only records.
- Internal metrics may overestimate real-world performance.
- No independent external evaluation dataset is included.

## Recommended improvements

1. Identify and document the original external source and licence.
2. Review the dataset for sensitive or identifying content.
3. Add verified `source`, `template_id`, and `is_synthetic` metadata.
4. Collect administrator-reviewed, privacy-preserving university examples under an approved process.
5. Evaluate separately on unseen real-world data before production use.
6. Add Arabic and mixed-language datasets with separate evaluation reports.

Internal evaluation results may not represent real-world university email performance. External validation is required before production use.
